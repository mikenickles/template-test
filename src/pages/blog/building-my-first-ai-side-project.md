Last weekend, I decided to build something small with AI. Not a startup. Not a product. Just a tool for myself — something that could summarize my reading notes into short, actionable takeaways.

## The idea

I read a lot. Books, articles, research papers. But my notes were scattered — some in Notion, some in Apple Notes, some scribbled in margins. I wanted a simple pipeline: paste in raw notes, get back a clean summary.

## What surprised me

The AI part was the easy part. Getting a language model to summarize text is almost trivial now. You write a prompt, call an API, and get back something useful.

The hard part was everything else:

- **Designing the input format.** My notes were inconsistent. Some were bullet points, some were full paragraphs, some were just single words that meant something only to me.
- **Deciding what "good" output looks like.** A summary can be too short, too long, too generic, or too detailed. Finding the right balance took more iteration than I expected.
- **The interface.** Even a "simple" tool needs some thought put into how you interact with it.

## What I'd do differently

I spent too long trying to make the prompts perfect before building anything visible. Next time, I'd start with the simplest possible UI and iterate from there.

> The best AI tools feel invisible. They don't show off — they just quietly make something easier.

## Takeaway

If you're curious about building with AI, just start. Pick something small and personal. The goal isn't to build the next big thing — it's to learn how these tools actually work when you move beyond the demo.
