I've tried every morning routine you can imagine. The 5 AM club. Cold showers. Meditation followed by journaling followed by exercise followed by a smoothie. They all lasted about two weeks before I was back to hitting snooze.

What finally worked was the opposite of what the productivity world tells you.

## Start ridiculously small

My entire morning routine is three things:

1. **Glass of water** — before anything else
2. **Ten minutes outside** — just standing, walking, or sitting
3. **One page of writing** — anything, no rules

That's it. No heroic wake-up time. No complicated stack of habits. Three things I can do even on my worst day.

## Why this works

The secret isn't the specific habits — it's the **consistency threshold**. When a routine is easy enough that you never skip it, something shifts. It stops being something you do and becomes something you are.

After three months of never missing a day, I naturally started waking up earlier. Not because I forced it, but because my body adjusted to the rhythm.

## The trap of optimization

The productivity community loves to optimize morning routines. Add this supplement. Try this breathing technique. Track these metrics.

But optimization is the enemy of consistency when you're starting out. You can't optimize something you haven't built yet.

> Consistency first. Optimization later. Most people have it backwards.

## Where I am now

Six months in, my mornings have naturally expanded. I often meditate. I usually exercise. But the core three remain non-negotiable — they're the foundation everything else is built on.

Start with less than you think you need. That's the whole secret.
