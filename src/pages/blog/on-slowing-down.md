There's a particular kind of anxiety that comes from feeling like you should always be doing more. Learning a new framework. Starting a side project. Building an audience. Shipping something.

I've been sitting with that anxiety lately instead of running from it.

## The cult of productivity

Somewhere along the way, rest became something you had to earn. Weekends became "catch-up" time. Hobbies became "content opportunities." Even meditation got turned into a productivity hack.

I don't think this is what a good life looks like.

## What slowing down means to me

It's not about doing nothing. It's about doing fewer things with more presence.

- Cooking a meal without listening to a podcast
- Going for a walk without tracking my steps
- Reading a book without thinking about how to summarize it
- Having a conversation without checking my phone

These sound simple. They're surprisingly hard.

## The practice of subtraction

Japanese aesthetics have a concept called *ma* (間) — the deliberate use of empty space. Not emptiness as absence, but emptiness as intention. The pause between notes in music. The white space on a page. The quiet in a conversation.

I've been trying to apply this to my life. Not adding more, but removing what doesn't serve me.

- Unfollowed accounts that made me feel behind
- Turned off all non-essential notifications
- Stopped saying yes to things out of obligation
- Let go of projects that were fueled by guilt, not passion

## The result

Nothing dramatic. No life-changing revelation. Just a little more peace. A little more space to think. A little more time for the things that actually matter.

> Sometimes the most productive thing you can do is stop producing.

That's enough for now.
